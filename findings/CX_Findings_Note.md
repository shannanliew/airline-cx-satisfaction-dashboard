# Customer Satisfaction: What's Driving It, and Where to Focus

*Based on 129,487 passenger surveys (public dataset, used here as a practice analysis — not airline-specific data).*

## The headline

Two things drive satisfaction more than anything else, and both are currently underperforming: **online boarding** and **inflight wifi**. Fixing either would move satisfaction more than any cabin-comfort improvement — seat comfort, food, and entertainment barely matter by comparison.

## What we found

**1. The digital experience matters more than the cabin experience.**
Online boarding and wifi are the two strongest drivers of whether a passenger ends up satisfied. They're also two of the worst-rated services overall (31–51% of passengers rate them positively) — high impact, currently broken, and the clear first priority.

**2. Personal travellers are a different problem than business travellers, and class upgrades don't fix it.**
Only about 1 in 10 personal travellers are satisfied, versus roughly 6 in 10 business travellers. Moving a personal traveller into a better class barely changes this. Whatever is driving their dissatisfaction isn't about the seat or the service level — it's something about the personal-travel experience itself that this data can't fully explain. Worth a short follow-up: a few exit interviews or one targeted survey question aimed specifically at personal travellers.

**3. "Loyal" and "disloyal" customers are almost entirely the same as business and personal travellers.**
Nearly every disloyal customer in this data is also a business traveller. A loyalty initiative and a business-traveller initiative would likely reach the same people — it's one problem, not two.

**4. Delays matter, but who they hurt depends on the traveller.**
Long delays cost business travellers more satisfaction, in absolute terms, than personal travellers — mostly because personal travellers are already starting from a low base. If delay-recovery resources (rebooking priority, compensation, lounge access) are limited, they protect more satisfaction when aimed at business travellers.

## What I'd do next

1. **Prioritize online boarding and wifi reliability** — the single highest-leverage fix available in this data.
2. **Treat personal-travel dissatisfaction as its own investigation**, not something a class upgrade solves.
3. **Report loyalty satisfaction split by travel type**, not as a separate number, since the two overlap almost completely.
4. **Point delay-recovery budget at business travellers first**, if resources are constrained.

## A caveat worth knowing

This model explains an unusually large share of satisfaction, more than a typical customer-satisfaction model does. That's likely because the dataset's satisfaction label was built partly from these same service ratings, not from something fully separate like "would you fly with us again." Treat the findings as directionally right, not as a precise real-world benchmark.
